Reading list

One main textbook, plus focused references for the machine-learning half. Chapters are given per lecture in each script.

Main textbook

  • [RN] S. Russell, P. Norvig: Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach, 4th edition, Pearson 2021. The standard AI textbook; covers lectures 1–6 and 12 in depth.

Machine learning and deep learning

  • [UDL] S. J. D. Prince: Understanding Deep Learning, MIT Press 2023. Free PDF at udlbook.github.io. Clear, modern, well illustrated; lectures 6–10.
  • [BB] C. M. Bishop, H. Bishop: Deep Learning: Foundations and Concepts, Springer 2024. More mathematical; good for probability and optimisation.
  • [GBC] I. Goodfellow, Y. Bengio, A. Courville: Deep Learning, MIT Press 2016. Free at deeplearningbook.org. Classic reference for lectures 7–9.

Language, reinforcement learning, agents

  • [JM] D. Jurafsky, J. H. Martin: Speech and Language Processing, 3rd edition draft. Free at web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3. Tokenisation, embeddings, Transformers, LLMs; lectures 10–11.
  • [SB] R. S. Sutton, A. G. Barto: Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, 2nd edition, MIT Press 2018. Free at incompleteideas.net; lecture 12.

Original papers worth reading

  • A. M. Turing (1950): Computing Machinery and Intelligence. Mind 59(236). Lecture 1.
  • D. E. Rumelhart, G. E. Hinton, R. J. Williams (1986): Learning representations by back-propagating errors. Nature 323. Lecture 8.
  • A. Krizhevsky, I. Sutskever, G. E. Hinton (2012): ImageNet Classification with Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (AlexNet). Lecture 9.
  • A. Vaswani et al. (2017): Attention Is All You Need. Lecture 10.
  • J. Kaplan et al. (2020): Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models; J. Hoffmann et al. (2022): Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (Chinchilla). Lecture 11.
  • L. Ouyang et al. (2022): Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback (InstructGPT). Lectures 11–12.
  • P. Lewis et al. (2020): Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks; S. Yao et al. (2023): ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models. Lecture 13.
