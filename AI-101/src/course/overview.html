Module AI-101 · 6 ECTS

Foundations of Artificial Intelligence

A one-semester introduction to artificial intelligence, from classical search and logic to the neural networks and large language models you run on your own hardware.

This course follows the shape of a typical first AI module at a European university: a weekly lecture, a weekly exercise session with problem sheets, a set of programming labs, and a written exam at the end. Every lecture comes as a self-contained script with learning objectives, numbered definitions and examples, a summary and exercises with model solutions. Terms with a highlighter mark, like rational agent, are in the glossary: hover over one to see its definition, click it to open the full entry.

Learning outcomes

After completing the module, students are able to:

  1. Describe AI problems in terms of agents, environments and performance measures, and choose a suitable family of methods (search, logic, probabilistic reasoning, learning).
  2. Apply uninformed and informed search, adversarial search and constraint propagation to well-defined problems, and argue about completeness, optimality and complexity.
  3. Represent knowledge in propositional and first-order logic and reason under uncertainty with Bayes' rule and Bayesian networks.
  4. Explain how supervised models are trained: loss functions, gradient descent, backpropagation, overfitting and regularisation.
  5. Analyse modern architectures, convolutional and recurrent networks and especially Transformers, and explain how large language models are pre-trained, aligned and served.
  6. Build small AI systems: a search agent, a classifier, a neural network from scratch, and a retrieval-augmented, tool-using agent on a local LLM.
  7. Evaluate AI systems critically: measurement, bias, safety and the limits of current methods.

How to work through it

  • One lecture per week. Read the script (about 90 minutes), then do the exercises before opening the solutions.
  • Labs every two weeks. Each lab builds on the lectures before it. Labs 5 and 6 use your own LM Studio server and LiteLLM router.
  • Mark lectures done with the button at the end of each one; your progress shows in the sidebar and stays in this browser.
  • Before the exam: work the sample exam under time pressure (90 minutes, no notes), then check the solutions.
